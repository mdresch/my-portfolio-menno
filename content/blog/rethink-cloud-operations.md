Moving to the cloud promised speed, elasticity, and efficiency. For many organizations, the reality has been more complicated. Workloads moved, but the operating model didn't. Incidents are still handled by hand, changes still wait in approval queues, security is still checked after the fact, and cloud bills keep growing without anyone knowing exactly why.

Running the cloud like a traditional data center wastes most of what the cloud can offer. This article outlines how to **rethink cloud operations**: an operating model built on automation, resilience, agile governance, and continuous optimization.

## Where Traditional Cloud Operations Fall Short

- **Manual, reactive operations:** incident detection, diagnosis, and resolution rely heavily on people, so issues take longer to fix and the same problems recur. In this case, incidents took around **45 minutes** to resolve and only about **30% of operational tasks** were automated.
- **Siloed governance:** separate teams for infrastructure, security, and finance, each with its own approvals.
- **Slow change management:** multiple layers of approval for routine changes, slowing delivery.
- **Decentralized cost management:** no central visibility, so resources sit underused — utilization was around **70%**.
- **Reactive security:** static, rule-based controls and manual compliance audits that can't keep up with a dynamic environment.
- **Innovation lag:** monolithic architectures and a lack of self-service meant new services took around **six months** to launch.

## The New Cloud Operating Model

### 1. Everything as Code

Infrastructure, configuration, policies, and runbooks are defined as code, version-controlled, reviewed, and deployed through automated pipelines. Environments become reproducible, changes become traceable, and drift becomes detectable.

### 2. Self-Healing Architectures

Systems are designed to recover on their own: health checks, auto-scaling, automatic failover across availability zones, and automated remediation for known issues. Engineers are alerted to what automation can't fix — not to every blip.

### 3. AI-Driven Operations

Machine learning detects anomalies, correlates events, predicts capacity needs, and recommends or performs fixes, reducing manual intervention and speeding up resolution.

### 4. Platform Engineering and Self-Service

A central platform team provides secure, compliant building blocks — "golden paths" for common workloads — that product teams can use on demand. Teams move fast because the guardrails are built into the platform.

### 5. Agile, Automated Governance

Instead of manual approval boards for every change, policies are encoded as automated checks ("policy as code"). Low-risk, standard changes flow through automatically; only genuinely risky changes need human review.

### 6. Security and Compliance by Design

Security controls, identity policies, encryption, and compliance checks are embedded in every layer and pipeline, with continuous monitoring and adaptive threat detection replacing periodic manual audits.

### 7. Continuous Cost Optimization

Real-time cost visibility, tagging, automated right-sizing, scheduling of non-production environments, and commitment management keep spending aligned with value — in close collaboration with finance (FinOps).

## Choosing the Path

The business case compared three options:

1. **Maintain the status quo:** no investment, but continued inefficiency, security exposure, cost overruns, and slow innovation.
2. **Incremental improvements:** targeted automation and tooling upgrades within the existing model — faster results, but the siloed governance and reactive culture remain.
3. **A modernized cloud operating model (recommended):** AI-driven automation, self-healing architectures, and agile governance combined into one adaptive operating model.

## Making the Transition

- **Start with a platform team** and a few willing product teams as early adopters.
- **Automate the most frequent and painful tasks first** to build credibility and free up capacity.
- **Redesign governance with risk, security, and audit at the table** so automation earns their trust.
- **Invest in skills:** infrastructure-as-code, automation, site reliability engineering, and cloud security.
- **Measure outcomes**, not activity: resolution times, availability, deployment speed, and cost efficiency.

## Measuring Success

| Metric | Baseline | Target |
| --- | --- | --- |

| Resource utilization | 70% | 90% |
| Security incidents | 12 a year | 4 a year |
| Time to market for new services | 6 months | 3 months |
| Automated operational tasks | 30% | 80% |
| Stakeholder satisfaction | 70% | 90% |

## Conclusion

The cloud's real advantages — speed, resilience, and efficiency — only materialize when the way it's operated changes too. Automation, self-healing design, platform engineering, policy as code, and continuous cost management turn cloud operations from a ticket-driven bottleneck into an enabler.

Rethinking cloud operations is as much about people and governance as technology. But organizations that make the shift spend less time firefighting, ship new services faster, and get far more value from every euro they spend in the cloud.
