Notion and Obsidian are two of the most loved productivity tools around — and two of the most intimidating. Both start as a blank page. Their flexibility is their superpower, but it also means many people spend entire weekends designing databases, linking notes, and watching tutorials before they get any real work done. Others give up halfway and end up with a beautiful but abandoned workspace.

That gap between potential and practice is an opportunity. This article outlines the **Notion/Obsidian Consultant**: a digital product and consulting business that creates modular, well-designed productivity dashboards — and helps people and teams adapt them to the way they actually work.

## Why Templates Often Disappoint

- **Generic, one-size-fits-all designs** that force users to adapt their workflow to the template instead of the other way round.
- **Poor modularity:** templates that work as a whole or not at all, and are hard to extend.
- **Weak integration** with calendars, task managers, and other tools, creating new silos.
- **No path from individual to team use.**
- **Inconsistent quality and documentation,** so users don't understand how a template is meant to work — and abandon it.
- **Customization is hard** for non-technical users, while fully custom builds are too expensive for most individuals.

## The Product: Modular Dashboards

The core offering is a library of high-quality dashboards built on a few principles:

- **Modular by design:** each dashboard is assembled from independent building blocks — tasks, projects, notes, goals, habits, reviews — that users can add, remove, or rearrange.
- **Best-practice UX:** clean layouts, clear navigation, and sensible defaults so users are productive on day one.
- **Grounded in proven methods:** second-brain approaches such as PARA, Zettelkasten-style linked notes, weekly reviews, and goal-to-task cascades — explained in plain language.
- **Well documented:** setup guides, short video walkthroughs, and example data showing how everything fits together.

### Two Platforms, Two Philosophies

| | Notion | Obsidian |
| --- | --- | --- |
| **Strength** | Databases, collaboration, and polished dashboards | Local-first Markdown notes, linking, and graph-based thinking |
| **Best for** | Project management, team workspaces, CRM-style tracking | Personal knowledge management, research, and writing |
| **Product format** | Duplicable workspace templates | Starter vaults with folder structures, templates, and recommended plugins |

### Use Cases

The initial library covers around ten dashboards across use cases such as **project management, knowledge management, habit tracking, goal setting, and team collaboration**, with niche editions for specific audiences — for example academic research or legal work — where generic templates fall short.

## The Business Model

Several revenue streams reinforce each other:

| Stream | Description |
| --- | --- |
| **Template sales** | One-time purchases at around $50 per template |
| **Premium subscription** | Ongoing updates, new modules, and member-only templates at around $10/month |
| **Custom development** | Tailored workspaces for individuals and teams, at around $1,000 per project |
| **Training and consulting** | Workshops and coaching on building a personal or team knowledge system |

Templates are the entry point. Customers who love them come back for premium modules, custom builds, and training — which is where much of the long-term value lies.

## The Business Case

| Option | Description | Indicative Cost | Trade-offs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Do nothing** | Don't enter the market. | None | No investment, but no revenue, audience, or authority |
| **2. Mid-range** | License existing templates, add basic customization and a small in-house library. | ~$50K upfront + ~$50K/year | Faster (3-6 months), but little differentiation, lower margins, and labor-intensive customization |
| **3. Full-scale custom library** *(recommended)* | An in-house library of modular templates, beta community, and multiple revenue streams. | ~$55K upfront + ~$55K/year | 6-9 months to launch, but differentiated, higher-margin, and scalable |

| 5-Year View | Option 2 | Option 3 |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Upfront investment | $50K | $55K |
| Operating cost (5-year) | $250K | $275K |
| Quantified benefits (5-year) | $600K | $1.2M |
| **Net value (5-year)** | **$300K** | **$870K** |

For only slightly more investment, owning the library outright — rather than reselling third-party templates — roughly triples the net value, because it removes licensing fees and builds a brand customers return to.

## Managing the Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Market saturation | High | Medium | Niche-specific templates and premium customization |
| Competitor response | High | Medium | Build brand authority and an engaged community |
| Low user adoption | Medium | High | Beta testing and A/B testing of pricing and positioning |
| High development costs | Medium | High | Agile development, freelancers, and a phased release |
| Platform changes | Medium | High | Track platform updates closely; keep templates simple and maintainable |
| Data privacy | Low | High | GDPR/CCPA-aware design, especially for team and client templates |

## Conclusion

The best productivity system is the one people actually use. Well-designed, modular dashboards remove the setup burden that stops so many people from getting value out of Notion and Obsidian — and give them a structure they can grow into.

For the creator, it's a business with attractive economics: products that are built once and sold many times, complemented by consulting and training for those who want a system tailored to them. Expertise in how people organize their work becomes something that scales.
