More and more travelers don't just want to see a place — they want to experience it. A cooking class in a family kitchen, a walking tour with a local historian, a morning with a craftsman in their workshop, a cycling route past the farms that supply the city's markets. These experiences are often the highlight of a trip.

Yet the people who offer them — independent guides, artisans, small businesses — usually struggle to reach travelers. They lack the time, skills, or budget for professional marketing and booking systems. Travelers, in turn, face a confusing mix of offers with very uneven quality.

This article outlines the **Local Experience Curator**: a business that designs, packages, and promotes curated local experiences and tours, connecting authentic local providers with the travelers looking for them.

## The Gap in the Market

- **Fragmented offerings:** local providers operate independently, with no shared quality standard or marketing reach.
- **Limited digital presence:** many providers have no proper website, booking system, or presence on the platforms travelers use.
- **Inconsistent quality:** travelers can't easily tell a great experience from a disappointing one.
- **Missed income:** without a scalable sales channel, local providers stay small and seasonal.
- **Manual operations:** bookings by phone and email, unanswered inquiries, and double bookings waste time on both sides.

## The Curator's Role

### 1. Discover and Design

Scout local guides, artisans, chefs, farmers, and cultural institutions, and work with them to design experiences with a clear story, schedule, group size, and price. The first goal is a portfolio of **ten market-ready experiences**.

### 2. Set Quality Standards

Every experience is tested before launch and meets clear standards for safety, accessibility, sustainability, and authenticity, with feedback after every booking to keep quality high.

### 3. Package and Present

Professional descriptions, photography, and consistent branding make each experience easy to understand and attractive to book. Experiences can be combined into themed packages — food, crafts, history, nature.

### 4. Distribute Through the Right Channels

Experiences are listed on ticketing and event platforms that travelers already use, as well as on the curator's own channels, with real-time availability and online payment.

### 5. Promote

Social media, content marketing, partnerships with hotels and tourist offices, and reviews from previous guests build demand.

### 6. Share the Value

Local partners receive a fair share of every booking. In the business case, the target is around **$300,000 a year** in direct income for local guides, artisans, and businesses.

## Build Options

| Option | Description | Indicative Investment | Trade-offs |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| **1. Status quo** | Providers keep working independently. | None | Lost bookings, inconsistent quality, and manual work |
| **2. Curation on an existing event platform** | List curated experiences on a ticketing platform, with light curation, templates, and marketing support. | ~$50K upfront + ~$120K/year | Fast (3-6 months) and low cost, but limited branding, data ownership, and features |
| **3. Own curated platform and brand** *(recommended in the business case)* | A branded booking platform with full control over experience design, customer data, and pricing. | ~$250K upfront | Higher investment, but stronger differentiation and lower transaction costs at scale |

A practical approach is to start on existing platforms to validate demand and refine the portfolio, then invest in an own platform once booking volumes justify it.

## Managing the Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| **Seasonality** | Mix indoor and outdoor experiences; target local residents and businesses off-season |
| **Quality varies between providers** | Testing before launch, standards, reviews, and coaching |
| **Insurance and regulatory requirements** | Clear agreements, liability coverage, and compliance checks per experience |
| **Dependence on platforms** | Build direct customer relationships and own channels |
| **Provider churn** | Fair revenue shares and genuine partnership |

## Measuring Success

| Metric | Target |
| --- | --- |
| Market-ready experiences | 10 in the first three months |
| Local partners onboarded | 15 in the first year |
| Bookings | 500 in the first six months; 20% monthly growth |
| Customer satisfaction | 90% |
| Repeat bookings | 30% within six months |
| Gross revenue | $1.2M over the first three years |

## Conclusion

The most memorable travel moments are usually created by local people sharing what they love. A curator who helps those people design great experiences, reach the right travelers, and run their bookings professionally creates value for everyone: richer trips for travelers, steady income for local providers, and a scalable business model that can be repeated in new regions.
