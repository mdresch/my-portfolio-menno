Email is still one of the highest-return channels in marketing — but only for the emails that arrive. When a meaningful share of messages lands in spam or never gets delivered at all, every campaign quietly underperforms, and the marketing team often doesn't even know why.

Mailbox providers have also raised the bar. Large providers now expect bulk senders to authenticate their mail properly, make unsubscribing easy, and keep spam complaints very low. Senders who don't comply see their mail filtered or rejected.

This article outlines an **Email Deliverability Consulting** engagement that tackles both halves of the problem: getting emails into the inbox, and designing templates that convert once they're there.

## Why Emails Don't Arrive

The issues usually fall into three groups.

### Technical

- **Missing or misconfigured authentication:** without correct **SPF**, **DKIM**, and **DMARC** records, mailbox providers can't verify that mail really comes from you — so they treat it with suspicion, and your domain is easier to spoof.
- **Poor list hygiene:** lists full of inactive, invalid, or unengaged addresses drive up bounces and complaints and damage sender reputation.
- **No monitoring:** without visibility into inbox placement and complaint rates, problems are discovered weeks too late.

### Operational

- **Siloed ownership:** marketing, IT, and compliance each own a piece of email, and nobody owns deliverability end to end.
- **Skills gaps:** teams lack specialist knowledge of authentication, reputation management, and list management.
- **Little testing:** subject lines, content, and send times are rarely A/B tested.

### Design

- **Templates that aren't mobile-first,** even though most emails are opened on phones.
- **Weak calls to action** and generic, unpersonalized content.
- **Inconsistent branding,** which undermines trust and recognition.

In the organization behind this business case, the symptoms were clear: an inbox placement rate of around **79%**, open rates of **12%**, click-through rates of **1.5%**, and an estimated 25-30% of the list inactive or invalid.

## The Engagement: Three Phases

### Phase 1 — Assessment and Strategy (Months 1-3)

A full **deliverability audit**: authentication records, sender reputation, blocklist status, bounce and complaint data, list quality, engagement by segment, and template performance. The audit produces a prioritized strategy and roadmap.

### Phase 2 — Implementation and Optimization (Months 4-7)

- **Authentication:** correct SPF, DKIM, and DMARC configuration — moving DMARC gradually toward enforcement — plus **BIMI** to display the brand logo in supporting inboxes.
- **List hygiene and segmentation:** remove invalid addresses, run re-engagement campaigns for inactive subscribers, sunset the unresponsive, and segment by engagement and interest.
- **Template redesign:** 10+ mobile-responsive, on-brand templates with clear calls to action and room for personalization.
- **Testing framework:** structured A/B tests for subject lines, content, design, and send times.

### Phase 3 — Monitoring and Continuous Improvement (Months 8-12)

Real-time monitoring of inbox placement, complaints, bounces, and engagement; training for marketing, IT, and compliance teams; and monthly performance reviews with optimization recommendations — so improvements stick after the consultants step back.

## Compliance Built In

Good deliverability and compliance go hand in hand. The engagement ensures consent, opt-out mechanisms, and sender identification meet the requirements of regulations such as **CAN-SPAM** and the **GDPR**, and that compliance has a seat at the table from the start.

## Conclusion

Deliverability is the invisible foundation of email marketing. Authentication, list hygiene, and reputation management aren't glamorous, but they determine whether anyone ever sees the carefully crafted campaign — and modern, mobile-first templates determine whether they act on it.

Fix the foundation, monitor it continuously, and keep testing: that's how email goes back to being one of the most profitable channels in the marketing mix.
