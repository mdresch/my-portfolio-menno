Satellite communications have changed dramatically. Newer medium-Earth-orbit (MEO) systems — such as O3b mPOWER, the class of network this article has in mind — deliver multiple gigabits per second, latency far closer to terrestrial networks than traditional geostationary satellites, and beams that can be steered and reconfigured as demand shifts. For cruise ships, offshore platforms, remote mines, government missions, mobile network backhaul, and cloud connectivity in hard-to-reach places, that opens possibilities that simply didn't exist before.

But raw capability isn't the same as customer value. Applications and services have to be designed, tested, and optimized to take advantage of what the network can do. This article outlines how an **Applications Center of Excellence (CoE)** can turn high-performance satellite capabilities into proven, repeatable solutions.

## The Problem: Capability Without Applications

- **Fragmented development:** applications that use the network are built in silos by different teams and partners, with inconsistent quality and duplicated effort.
- **Underused capabilities:** features such as uncontended bandwidth, predictable latency, and dynamic routing remain unused when applications aren't designed for them.
- **Slow, inconsistent delivery:** no standard approach for testing, validation, and deployment.
- **Missed opportunities:** without packaged solutions, customers in latency-sensitive industries keep relying on alternatives they consider more proven.

## What the Center of Excellence Does

### 1. Governance and Best Practices

A central team defines reference architectures, design patterns, and performance guidelines for building applications that make the most of satellite capacity and latency characteristics.

### 2. Industry Solutions

The CoE develops and packages solutions for priority sectors:

| Sector | Example Applications |
| --- | --- |
| **Maritime and cruise** | Passenger connectivity, crew welfare, vessel operations and telemetry |
| **Energy and mining** | Remote operations, real-time monitoring, video and safety systems |
| **Government and defense** | Secure, resilient communications in remote areas |
| **Telecom operators** | Mobile backhaul to extend coverage to underserved regions |
| **Cloud and enterprise** | Direct, low-latency connectivity to cloud platforms from remote sites |
| **Healthcare** | Telemedicine in remote and disaster-hit locations |

### 3. Testing and Validation

A test environment that simulates real network conditions lets applications be validated for latency, throughput, failover, and security before customers depend on them.

### 4. Dynamic Network Integration

APIs and tooling let applications and customers request, route, and rebalance bandwidth dynamically — turning network flexibility into a feature customers can actually use.

### 5. Partner Ecosystem

Collaboration with technology partners, cloud providers, system integrators, and customers accelerates development and brings solutions to market faster.

### 6. Knowledge Sharing

Documentation, training, and reusable components ensure that what one team learns benefits everyone.

## A Phased Approach

| Phase | Focus |
| --- | --- |
| **1. Establish** | Team, governance, reference architectures, and test environment |
| **2. Pilot** | A few flagship applications with lead customers in priority sectors |
| **3. Scale** | Expand the solution portfolio and partner ecosystem |
| **4. Optimize** | Continuous improvement based on performance data and customer feedback |

## Managing the Risks

| Risk | Mitigation |
| --- | --- |
| **CoE becomes a bottleneck** | Enable teams with standards and tools rather than centralizing all development |
| **Solutions don't match customer needs** | Co-develop with lead customers in each sector |
| **Technology changes quickly** | Modular architectures and regular reviews |
| **Security and regulatory requirements** | Security by design and compliance checks in validation |
| **Unclear value** | Track adoption, revenue, and network utilization from the start |

## Measuring Success

| Metric | Example Target |
| --- | --- |
| Applications developed and validated | 10 within two years |
| Time to market per application | Under six months |
| Customer satisfaction | 90% positive feedback |
| Network capacity utilization | 80% or higher |
| Compliance with applicable regulations | 100% |

## Conclusion

Advanced satellite networks can bring high-performance connectivity to places terrestrial networks will never reach. But the value is realized through applications that are designed for the network, validated under real conditions, and packaged for specific industries. An Applications Center of Excellence provides the governance, expertise, and reusable building blocks to make that happen consistently — turning network capability into customer outcomes.
