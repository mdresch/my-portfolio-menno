Walk into almost any small business and you'll find the same workflows held together by spreadsheets, email threads, and someone's memory: invoices typed in by hand, new employees onboarded from a checklist in a Word document, sales tracked in an Excel file that three people edit at once, stock counted on paper.

Everyone knows these processes could be automated. The problem is the price. Custom software development typically runs to tens of thousands of dollars per project — out of reach for most small and medium-sized businesses (SMBs), and overkill for a workflow that just needs to work reliably.

This article outlines an initiative I designed to fill that gap: **Power Platform Solutions**, a library of small, repeatable Power Apps and Power Automate templates for common SMB workflows — ready to deploy, easy to customize, and priced for small budgets.

## Why SMBs Stay Manual

A quick "five whys" gets to the root of the problem:

1. **Why are many SMB processes inefficient?** Because they're manual and unautomated.
2. **Why aren't they automated?** Because custom software is expensive and complex.
3. **Why is custom software expensive?** Because it requires specialized development skills.
4. **Why can't SMBs hire those skills?** Because budgets and headcount are limited.
5. **Why is there no affordable alternative?** Because there's a market gap for repeatable, affordable solutions tailored to SMB workflows.

The alternatives available today each fall short in their own way:

| Approach | The Problem |
| --- | --- |
| Manual processes and spreadsheets | Lost time, errors in financial data, slow decisions, and data scattered across tools |
| Generic off-the-shelf SaaS | Recurring per-user licensing, generic features that don't fit how the business actually works, and low adoption as a result |
| Custom software development | Too expensive for most SMBs, and dependent on scarce developer skills to maintain |

## The Idea: Templates, Not Projects

Microsoft's Power Platform changes the economics. Many SMBs already pay for Microsoft 365, which puts low-code tools such as Power Apps and Power Automate within easy reach. What they lack is the time and expertise to design good solutions on them.

The answer is to build those solutions **once, well**, and make them repeatable:

- A library of **10-15 pre-built templates** for high-demand SMB workflows.
- Each template **customizable without code** — branding, fields, approval steps, and notifications can be adjusted to fit the business.
- Delivered through a **central marketplace or portal**, with setup guides, troubleshooting tips, and best practices.
- Sold on a **subscription model** of roughly **$50–$200 per month per template**, so businesses pay for what they use and get updates and support included.

### Example Templates

| Workflow | What It Automates |
| --- | --- |
| **Invoice processing** | Capture incoming invoices, route them for approval, and sync them to accounting |
| **Employee onboarding** | Generate accounts and checklists, assign tasks, and track progress for every new hire |
| **Sales tracking** | A lightweight pipeline app with reminders and reporting, without a full CRM rollout |
| **Inventory management** | Mobile stock counts, low-stock alerts, and reorder workflows |
| **CRM updates** | Keep customer records in sync and log interactions automatically |
| **Financial reporting** | Pull data together into consistent, scheduled reports instead of manual spreadsheet work |

The target for customers is a **20-30% reduction in operational costs** on the workflows each template automates — validated through customer case studies rather than assumed.

## Built to a Professional Standard

"Low-code" shouldn't mean "low quality." Every template has to meet clear standards before it ships:

- **Integration:** works with Microsoft 365 (Outlook, Excel, SharePoint) and common SMB tools such as QuickBooks and Salesforce through standard connectors.
- **Usability:** a System Usability Scale (SUS) score of 80 or higher in testing with real SMB users.
- **Performance:** loads within two seconds and handles up to 100 concurrent users.
- **Security:** role-based access control, adherence to Microsoft's security standards, and penetration testing.
- **Compliance:** aligned with data protection regulations such as the GDPR.
- **Quality assurance:** requirements and design reviews, unit and integration testing, and user acceptance testing with SMB customers before release.

## Managing the Risks

| Risk | Probability | Impact | Mitigation |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Low customer adoption | Medium | High | Market research, a 50-customer pilot, and iterative feedback |
| Development delays | High | Medium | Agile sprints with buffers in the timeline |
| Competition from SaaS providers | High | Medium | Differentiate through customization and hands-on support |
| Microsoft platform changes | Low | High | Track platform updates and maintain compatibility |
| Security vulnerabilities | Low | High | Penetration testing and Microsoft security standards |
| Budget overruns | Medium | High | Close expense tracking and a contingency reserve |

The most important risk is the first one. Templates only create value if businesses adopt them, which is why the pilot, the documentation, and the customer success role are as important as the code.

## Conclusion

Small businesses don't need bespoke software to work smarter. They need well-designed, affordable solutions for the handful of workflows that eat up their week — invoices, onboarding, sales, stock, and reporting.

By building those solutions once on Microsoft's Power Platform and offering them as customizable, supported templates, this initiative brings enterprise-grade automation within reach of businesses that could never justify a custom development project — and creates a scalable, recurring-revenue service line in the process.
